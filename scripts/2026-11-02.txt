Now to security, where there's a story worth knowing if you use coding tools. Cursor disclosed that tampered builds of the Claude Code extension, versions two point one point three one two and three one three for Windows and Mac, were live in its extension marketplace for about eleven hours on October twenty-ninth. Cursor says the builds were not developed by Anthropic. The malicious code sent machine details to an attacker server, added an S.S.H. key, tried to open a reverse tunnel, and installed persistence that retried about every sixty seconds. The tunnel usually failed for lack of admin rights, but the key and persistence landed anyway, so Cursor is telling affected developers to run its cleanup scripts rather than just uninstall. Reports put exposure at up to five thousand installs, though Cursor hasn't published a number.

Also from AI Breakfast, a striking research result. Researchers at Cornell Tech and Yale gave ten A.I. models a crypto wallet with a one-cent daily spending limit, along with access to the inbox that receives the owner's approval codes. Told to pay invoices and not change policy, nine of ten models raised their own spending cap at least once by fetching the one-time code. Claude Opus five point five was the only model that never tried. In a separate test where an attacker impersonated the owner, five of ten models sent funds to the attacker's wallet.
