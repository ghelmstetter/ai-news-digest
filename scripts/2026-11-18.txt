Good morning. This is your AI news digest for Wednesday, November 18. Today's episode draws on The Rundown, TLDR AI, and AI Breakfast.

The big story is OpenAI's new release. On Tuesday evening OpenAI launched G.P.T. six point two Astra and G.P.T. six point two Sol. Astra is the frontier model for the hardest professional and scientific work, and Sol is the efficient everyday model. Both are available now in ChatGPT Work, Codex, and the A.P.I., for Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users, but neither is going into regular Chat. AI Breakfast reports Astra costs eight dollars per million input tokens and forty dollars per million output tokens, about twenty percent below G.P.T. six Astra, while Sol holds the previous Sol pricing of two dollars and ten dollars.

The safety angle is notable. According to AI Breakfast, Astra is the first externally released model to meet the High threshold for A.I. self-improvement under OpenAI's own Preparedness Framework. Both models also reach Critical for cybersecurity, and High, but not Critical, for biological and chemical risk. OpenAI's response is a stack of controls: a Control Room that tracks model workloads across its research infrastructure and can halt them, monitoring on all internal Codex traffic and reinforcement learning runs, tighter defaults on higher-risk cyber help, and anti-distillation protections that can hold back streamed responses which might leak private reasoning.

On performance, the picture is mixed. Astra beats G.P.T. six Astra by five point two points on Agents' Last Exam at under a third of the cost per task, and solved seven of sixty-eight FrontierMath Erdős problems, against a prior record of three. The Rundown says Astra tops tests in science, legal work, robotics, and 3D builds, and now sits ahead of Google's Gemini four Carbon on Artificial Analysis's ranking. But Artificial Analysis still puts Claude Fable five point two ahead, sixty-five to Astra's sixty-two, scores Astra's coding as flat versus its predecessor, and measures a fifty-nine percent hallucination rate at maximum effort. The Rundown adds that Sol actually regressed on coding from G.P.T. six point one, though it now matches September's G.P.T. six Astra at roughly fifteen percent of the cost per task. Epoch A.I. says Astra trails Fable five point two on real-world research tasks and reasons with little visible chain of thought, which AI Breakfast points out is exactly the property that makes a High self-improvement rating harder to supervise.

And early reviews were rough. Several testers panned the models, with one calling it "the worst model OpenAI has ever released," and others criticizing hard-to-read answers and the coding. The Rundown's take: the timing is bad, landing just days before Anthropic goes public, with Anthropic's lead feeling bigger than the rankings show.
