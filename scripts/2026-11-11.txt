That brings us to Google. Gemini four Carbon is now generally available, two weeks after it was announced. TLDR A.I. says it's open to all paying developers and enterprises through the A.P.I., and rolling out to Google A.I. Ultra subscribers in Antigravity, the Gemini app, and A.I. Mode in Search. Interestingly, Google says most people should not use Carbon, recommending Argon for ordinary software and knowledge work, because Carbon burns far more tokens reasoning and checking its work.

Google also previewed a feature called swarm in Antigravity, which runs a team of named agents that split up a task, work in parallel, and coordinate on a shared message board. TLDR A.I. warns that each worker is its own agent, so swarms use up quotas much faster. And Google Cloud launched a U.S. preview of Gemini for Healthcare, with connectors to medical records and claims systems, and partners including Northwell, H.C.A., and Optum.

Next, OpenAI. The company introduced Businesses in ChatGPT, a beta where brands can claim a verified profile, update their information, and provide product feeds with pricing and availability. Eligible U.S. online retailers and digital service firms can also launch business agents that answer shopper questions in a labeled chat, separate from ChatGPT's own answers, and brands can now pay to make that agent the landing spot for ads in ChatGPT. The Rundown frames it as the A.I.-era version of fighting for a spot in Google's search results. Separately, OpenAI acquired Odessia, a months-old A.I. travel planning startup from Sonder founder Francis Davidson, in what TLDR A.I. says appears to be an acqui-hire.
